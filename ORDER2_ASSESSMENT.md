# Wardrobe Assessment — Nordstrom Order 7/22/2026

14 items, $2,062 in line-item prices (order total $2,084.87 after tax, with $975.13 in Nordstrom Notes/rewards applied). Below is a quality, brand, and value read on each, grouped by how confident I am that it was a good buy.

**Important pricing note:** Two of the priciest items were bought *below* full MSRP — the Jack Victor tees ($228 retail → paid $149.99) and the AG Airluxe pants ($215–225 retail → paid $149.99). This suggests the Nordstrom prices were sale/reduced prices, not MSRP, on at least part of the order. Where I know MSRP I've noted it; where I don't, I mark it as unverified rather than guess.

---

## The strongest buys

**Jack Victor Wellington Cotton & Silk Tee — Dark Aviator + Espresso ($149.99 each)**
Best value in the order. These retail at **$228 direct** from Jack Victor, so ~$150 is a real discount (~34% off). Jack Victor is a respected Montreal tailoring house; 70% cotton / 30% silk gives a tee a subtle sheen and drape that a cotton tee can't match. The catch: **dry-clean only.** A $150 tee you can't machine wash is a commitment — plan to wear an undershirt or accept dry-cleaning costs over its life. Buying two colors of the same proven piece is a sensible move. Quality: high. Value: strong (at this price).

**AG Everett Airluxe Commuter Pants — Bering Sea + Chalk ($149.99 each)**
AG (Adriano Goldschmied) is a premium denim/pant brand; the Airluxe Commuter line retails **$215–225** and reviews well (4.6/5 on AG's site). 53% cotton / 44% poly / 3% elastane is a performance stretch-sateen — machine washable, wrinkle-resistant, good for travel and long days. At ~$150 these are a discount on a genuinely versatile smart-casual pant. Two neutral colors (a dark and a near-white) is a smart pairing. Quality: high. Value: strong.

**AG Everett Slim Straight Jeans — 7 Years Sulfur Willowed ($149.99)**
Same brand pedigree. 93% cotton / 6% poly / 1% elastane is a premium stretch denim, machine washable. $150 is the normal AG jeans price point — fair, not a steal, but a reliable core denim. Quality: high. Value: fair.

## Solid, no concerns

**rag & bone Oliver Crewneck Sweater — Fog ($198.99)**
rag & bone is a well-regarded contemporary brand. The fabric is 64% cotton / 28% viscose / 8% polyamide — a soft, drapey blend rather than pure natural fiber, which is why it's washable (dry clean or machine wash, dry flat). At ~$199 it's priced like a premium cotton-blend sweater. Fine buy; just note it's a blend, not a wool or all-cotton piece. Quality: solid. Value: fair.

**rag & bone Infuse Denim Button-Up — Luke ($178.99)**
100% cotton denim shirt, machine washable. rag & bone's denim expertise shows here. ~$179 is toward the top of the range for a denim shirt, but the "infused" softening process and brand justify some of it. Quality: solid. Value: fair.

**rag & bone Fit 3 Authentic Jeans (Greenpoint, $159.99) + Fit 3 Aero Jeans (Camel, $139.99)**
Both are athletic-fit stretch jeans (99% cotton/1% elastane and 91% cotton/8% poly/1% elastane respectively). The Fit 3 is cut for athletic builds — more room in seat/thigh. Standard rag & bone pricing, machine washable. Two different washes/fabrics, so not redundant. Quality: solid. Value: fair.

**Vince Pima Cotton & Modal Henley — Coastal ($129.99)**
Vince is a quality elevated-basics brand. 60% pima cotton / 40% modal is a soft, slightly lustrous, drapey blend — a step up from a basic henley. Machine washable, made in Peru. ~$130 is standard for Vince. Quality: solid. Value: fair.

**NN07 Kaley Cotton Crewneck Sweater — Denim Blue ($129.99)**
NN07 (No Nationality 07) is a Danish brand known for clean, well-made basics. 100% cotton, machine washable, slim fit with nice saddle-shoulder detailing. ~$130 is on-brand pricing. A dependable lightweight layering sweater. Quality: solid. Value: fair.

**rag & bone Garment Dye Polo — Mist Green ($98.99)**
100% cotton, garment-washed for a broken-in feel, machine washable. Lowest-priced item and the least to overthink — a good everyday polo from a strong brand. Quality: solid. Value: good.

**Robert Barakett Satoru Short-Sleeve Button-Up — Black ($148.00)**
Robert Barakett is a Canadian brand known for pima-cotton softness. 97% cotton / 3% elastane stretch, textured stripe, machine wash (line dry). A comfortable warm-weather shirt. ~$148 is a touch high for a short-sleeve cotton shirt, but the fabric and stretch are nicer than typical. Quality: solid. Value: fair.

## The one to scrutinize

**Robert Barakett Croft Textured Shirt Jacket — Moonstone ($149.99)**
This is the only item where the fabric doesn't match the price. It's **100% polyester** (a textured seersucker), lined, dry-clean only. Polyester is the least premium fiber in the entire order, yet it's priced the same as the silk-blend tees and the AG performance pants. You're paying for the brand, the styling, and the shirt-jacket versatility rather than the material. It'll look sharp and it's a useful layering piece — but of the 14, it's the weakest value on a fabric-per-dollar basis. If it hadn't been part of a larger order, I'd have flagged it to reconsider. Quality: fabric is the low point; construction/design fine. Value: weakest in the order.

---

## Overall read

Strong, coherent order. It's a smart-casual, brand-forward wardrobe built on reputable labels (rag & bone, AG, Vince, NN07, Jack Victor, Robert Barakett) with sensible color choices and useful duplicates (two AG pant colors, two Jack Victor tee colors). Most pieces are machine washable; the exceptions to remember are the **two Jack Victor tees and the Croft shirt jacket (all dry-clean)**.

Value-wise: the AG pants and Jack Victor tees were the best deals (bought below MSRP). Most rag & bone / Vince / NN07 items were bought at normal price — fair, not bargains. The one genuine question mark is the **$150 all-polyester Croft shirt jacket**, where the price outruns the fabric.

If you want to be more deliberate going forward, the pattern worth watching is paying $130–150 for pieces where the fabric is a synthetic or a blend — that's where the brand premium is doing most of the work.
